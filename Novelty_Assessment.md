# Honest Novelty Assessment — vs. the Sonti-group line of work

*Based on web searches of the relevant literature (June 2026). This is a desk assessment, not an
exhaustive review; a final check should include reading the full PDFs of the papers below and a
citation scan of Sarkar & Sonti (2007).*

## The Sonti-group asymptotic-tracking line (what already exists)

1. **Sarkar & Sonti (2007), JSV 306:657** — the base paper. Single flexible plate + rigid cover,
   asymptotic ε-series catalogue of coupled wavenumbers. (Quiescent.)
2. **Sarkar, Kunte & Sonti (2011), CMES 81:249** — "Unified Dispersion Characteristics." Shows the
   coupled dispersion equation of *infinite flexible structural-acoustic waveguides* conforms to a
   **generic form**, demonstrated for **rectangular, circular-cylindrical and elliptical** geometries,
   with all coupled-wavenumber solutions on a single schematic. (Quiescent.)
3. **Sarkar & Sonti (2011), JSV 330** — generalized asymptotic wavenumber expansions for in-vacuo
   orthotropic cylindrical shells.
4. **Prakash & Sonti (2016), JSV 373:164** — "A systematic asymptotic approach … with **arbitrary
   fluid loading**," i.e. the small-/large-ε catalogue generalized to any fluid loading. (Quiescent.)

**Conclusion on the quiescent part of our work.** The two-flexible-plate symmetric/antisymmetric
asymptotic catalogue — while we derived and validated it independently and correctly — is **largely
subsumed** by this body of work:
- the symmetric/antisymmetric split for two identical flexible walls is classical (Fahy);
- a flexible-walled **rectangular** duct is one of the three geometries already unified in CMES 2011;
- arbitrary-ε behaviour is exactly Prakash & Sonti 2016.
A referee from this community would regard our quiescent catalogue as a **clean special case /
re-derivation**, not a new result. Novelty here: **low**.

## Where our work *is* plausibly novel

No paper found in this line (or, in my searches, elsewhere) adds a **mean flow** to the
asymptotic-tracking framework for a flexible-plate waveguide. Our genuinely new content is:
1. **Low-Mach Poiseuille mean flow** in the two-flexible-plate waveguide via the Pridmore–Brown
   equation with a **plate-admittance** boundary condition;
2. the **closed-form O(M) correction** (Fredholm solvability) and the **O(M²) convection/shear split**
   for every coupled branch;
3. the **critical-layer threshold M_crit = Ω/ξ** for this system and the finding (inviscid + viscous)
   that the **discrete wall modes are not absorbed** by the critical layer (Stokes-dominated decay);
4. the **convective-stability** result (no flutter) and the **full nonlinear-M dispersion maps**.

**Important caveat.** Sheared-flow duct acoustics with *impedance/compliant* walls (Pridmore–Brown +
admittance BC + critical layer) is a **mature field** (Pridmore-Brown 1958; Brambley, Darau &
Rienstra 2012; Rienstra; and the AIAA Pridmore-Brown literature). A flexible plate is mathematically a
particular frequency/wavenumber-dependent wall admittance. So the **critical-layer phenomenology
itself is well established**; our novelty is the **combination** (flexible-plate/Sonti-style asymptotic
tracking + Poiseuille profile) and the specific closed-form low-Mach corrections and wall-mode
results — **moderate, incremental novelty**, not a new phenomenon.

## Recommendation for framing

The paper should **lead with the mean-flow contribution** as the core novelty and present the
quiescent two-plate catalogue as *setup that recovers and extends known results* (citing CMES 2011 and
Prakash & Sonti 2016 explicitly). Claiming the two-plate catalogue as a primary novelty would invite
a "this is already in refs [Unified 2011 / Prakash–Sonti 2016]" rejection. Framed honestly around the
mean-flow + critical-layer + stability results, it is a credible **incremental** contribution suitable
for JSV / Wave Motion / JASA — but not a "first/only" claim.

## Bottom line

- Quiescent two-plate catalogue: **not novel** (subsumed by Sonti-group 2011/2016).
- Mean-flow (Poiseuille/Pridmore–Brown) extension + low-Mach closed forms + critical-layer/stability
  results for the flexible-plate waveguide: **plausibly novel as a combination**, moderate
  significance, with the critical-layer machinery itself borrowed from the mature lined-duct literature.
- Honest verdict: a **publishable incremental paper if reframed around the flow results**; not a
  guaranteed-original or high-impact result. Read the four papers above in full before any novelty
  claim in a cover letter.
